# Rebuilt the hiragana s-row, but remembers it by rote and can't yet state the pattern

After a first diagnostic (3 of 10, then 6 of 8), the learner rebuilt the s-row and got all five right in a scrambled check (sa, shi, su, se, so). They had been swapping す and し. That was recall within a minute of practice, not yet storage. Their explanation of how they remember it was "the more complicated one comes first" (in すし, す then し) and, asked for the pattern behind the row, "not sure, I just remember it". See [gaps.md](../gaps.md).

## Evidence
- Scrambled s-row check, 5 of 5, immediately after teaching.
- Explain-back, in the learner's words: "Remember the more complicated one comes first." Corrected and extended: the rule works when tied to shape (the more complicated character, す, is su; the simple hook, し, is shi). The row pattern is a consonant plus the vowel order a, i, u, e, o, with し as the exception ("shi", not "si"). The learner could not state the pattern unprompted.
- Earlier in the session: solid on あ, い, お, か, け, き, し, た, ん; unremembered: て, ぬ, ふ, ま, よ, れ.

## Implications
- Re-test the s-row in scrambled order, す vs し, and ask the learner to state the row pattern from memory before moving on.
- Next: the t-row and the characters not yet placed (ぬ, ふ, ま, よ, れ). The learner's sounds are attached to the wrong shapes in places, so check both directions: shape to sound.
- Their first Japanese word is すし, which can anchor the pattern.

Re-test: on or after 2026-11-21 (the scrambled s-row, す vs し, and the row pattern from memory).
